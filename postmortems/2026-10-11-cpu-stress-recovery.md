# Postmortem: CPU Stress and Workload Recovery — 2026-10-11

**Status:** Resolved
**Experiment:** Chaos Mesh `StressChaos` targeting one `sparebox-web` pod
**Blast radius:** Namespace `gitops-test`; one web pod
**Duration:** Configured stress duration of 30 seconds; exact observed start and end times not recorded
**MTTR:** Not measured; no alert-to-resolution timestamps were collected

## Summary

A controlled CPU stress experiment targeted one `sparebox-web` pod,
using one CPU worker at 25% load for 30 seconds. Chaos Mesh reported
successful fault injection and recovery in Kubernetes events. The
Deployment remained at two ready replicas, and the temporary
StressChaos resource was deleted after testing.

The experiment validated fault injection and workload recovery at the
Kubernetes level. It did not measure HTTP performance or user-visible
impact during the stress interval.

## Root cause

The CPU load was intentionally generated by Chaos Mesh as a controlled
resilience experiment. It was not an unexpected infrastructure failure.

## Impact

- **Workload:** `sparebox-web`
- **Namespace:** `gitops-test`
- **Failure mode:** CPU stress on one selected pod
- **Configured load:** One CPU worker at 25% for 30 seconds
- **Replica availability:** Deployment remained at 2/2 ready replicas
- **HTTP impact:** Unknown; no HTTP probe measurements were recorded during injection
- **Error budget consumed:** Not calculated

## What the experiment proved, and what it did not

**Proved**
- Chaos Mesh successfully injected the configured CPU stress.
- Chaos Mesh reported successful recovery.
- The Deployment remained at two ready replicas.
- The temporary StressChaos resource was removed after testing.

**Not proved**
- HTTP availability or latency throughout the stress interval.
- Whether CPU pressure affected request processing.
- Exact recovery duration or MTTR.
- Resilience to sustained CPU saturation or simultaneous multi-pod stress.
- A quantified SLO or error-budget impact.
